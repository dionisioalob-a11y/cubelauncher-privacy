Cube Launcher Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 November 2026

Cube Launcher is a home screen app. It does not collect or sell any of your data, and the developer runs no server. It has no ads and no analytics. A few optional features you turn on send the data they need to an outside service, listed under Online features. Nothing else leaves your phone through the app.

What the app uses, and why

  • Your list of installed apps: to show them on the puzzle and in All apps, and to open them.
  • Contacts (only if you allow it): when you type in search, matching contact names are shown so you can open them. Android asks your permission the first time. Contacts are read only while you search and are never stored.
  • Notifications (only if you turn on Notification access): to show dots on apps with notifications and music controls for what is playing. If you separately enable the themed notification panel, it also displays notification titles and messages in your launcher theme. Up to 50 previews, each field limited to 1000 characters, are held only in process memory; they are never saved to disk or sent over the network. Hidden apps, child mode, secret notifications and locked-device previews are excluded. Previews clear when the screen locks, access disconnects or you turn off the panel. Opening or dismissing a notification happens only when you tap it. Turning off the panel leaves Android access available for dots until you revoke it in Settings.
  • Accessibility service (only if you turn it on): used to lock the screen when you double-tap empty space, and to show the cube lock screen when the phone wakes, if you turn that on. It cannot read what is on your screen.
  • Fingerprint, face or screen lock: used through Android's own lock screen check to protect hidden apps and child mode. The app never sees your fingerprint, face, PIN or password.
  • Widgets you add: shown on your home screen by Android. The app doesn't read their content.
  • Calendar (only if you allow it): to show your upcoming events on the Calendar card. Events are read when the card is shown and are never stored or sent.
  • Steps from Health Connect (only if you allow it): to show today's and this week's step count on the Steps card. Step counts are read only, shown on your phone and never stored or sent. The app does not write to Health Connect.
  • App usage (only if you turn on Usage access): to show screen time on the Phone Status card. It stays on your phone.
  • Location (only if you allow it and turn on the 📍 option in Traffic): to show traffic from where you are now. See Online features.
  • Notifications and alarms: to send leave-now commute alerts, reminders you set, class reminders from your timetable, the end of a focus session, and weather and storm warnings for your weather cities.
  • Do Not Disturb (only if you allow it): the Focus timer turns on Do Not Disturb (priority only) during a focus session and turns it back off when the session ends, or when a class is about to start. It does not read your notifications.

Online features

These work only after you turn them on. Each sends only what it needs, directly from your phone to that service. That service's own privacy policy applies.

  • Weather (Open-Meteo): the city name you search for, then that city's coordinates, to get the forecast and today's wind and rain warnings. While a city is saved, its weather is also checked in the background about every 2 hours, for storm warnings. If you search by postal code, or for a place Open-Meteo doesn't know (such as a mall or business district), the search and country are looked up by your phone's built-in location service (on most phones, Google's), and Google's privacy policy applies to that search.
  • Storms (GDACS, the UN and European Commission disaster alert system): while a weather city is shown, the app downloads the public list of active tropical storms worldwide. Nothing about you or your city is sent; the distance to each storm is worked out on your phone.
  • Traffic and commute (TomTom): the Home and Work places you enter (searched as text, then as coordinates), and your current location when the 📍 option is on, to find routes, delays and incidents. A traffic key you add is stored only on your phone and is never included in backups.
  • Orbit AI: only text you type and submit is sent, to the AI provider address you enter yourself. Your provider key is kept in memory and cleared when the app closes.
  • Cube Launcher Pro (Google Play): purchases are handled by Google Play; the app only learns whether you own Pro.
  • Maps and web search: opening a route or a search hands it to your maps app or browser.

What is stored on your phone

Your settings (layout, folders, dock, theme, wallpaper, solve times, which apps you open most, Home and Work places, weather cities, step goal, class timetable, focus sessions, apps you hide during focus and the last traffic result) are stored inside the app on your phone. They are deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

If you use Back up settings, the app writes a file to the place you choose. That file contains your settings, the names of your apps, your Home and Work places, your weather cities, your class timetable and the apps you hide during focus, but no contacts, notifications, steps, calendar events or keys. Keep it private: it shows which apps you use and where you live and work.

Sharing

The developer receives none of your data. The only sharing is what the online features above send when you use them.

Children

Child mode limits which apps a child can open. It collects no information about the child.
